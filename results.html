Results

The box went up in October 2026, so the honest summary is: the technology works, the birds have not moved in yet.

What works

  • Camera and IR light give a clear black-and-white picture of the inside of the box, day and night.
  • The Pi stays around 50 °C in continuous operation and does not throttle.
  • BirdNET-Pi names wild birds around the house from the first day.
  • A finger held in front of the lens triggers the push message, switches "bird present" on, and after the timeout off again.
  • The live picture runs on iPhone and Apple TV through HomeKit.

What is not proven yet

  • Real birds in the box. The detection thresholds are first guesses.
  • How often the model alone recognises a bird at the top edge of the picture.
  • How warm the IR module gets in summer.

Lessons so far

  • Motion is a better signal than the model when the animal is only partly in view. Combine both.
  • Use a high-endurance SD card. The Pi writes pictures and logs all day.
  • Back up the card as an image before changing anything big (dd on a Mac or Linux machine works well and needs no extra software), and keep the old card as a spare.
  • Keep secrets out of scripts. Webhook address in a service drop-in, passwords changed on the Pi and in BirdNET-Pi.
  • Start every library with its telemetry off.

Next steps

  1. Let it run over the winter and collect pictures (they are labelled bird and nobird).
  2. In spring: tune the thresholds and the "empty after" time (300 seconds is a likely candidate), and review false alarms.
  3. If the model misses too many birds, retrain it with my own pictures, labelled locally on a computer, without any cloud service.
  4. Watch the IR module's temperature in summer.
  5. Maybe: the frogs in the garden pond, and an ultrasound microphone for bats.
